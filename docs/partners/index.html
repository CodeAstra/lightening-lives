For hospitals and laboratories. Confirmed diagnosis, in your own laboratory.

Run screening and confirmation in one test, on a dried blood spot, with the PCR equipment you already have.

What changes for your laboratory

  • No DNA extraction. A punch from the card goes straight into the reaction.

  • Samples arrive at room temperature. Dried cards need no cold chain.

  • One test instead of two. Screening and confirmation in a single run.

  • High throughput. Many cards in one run.

  • Results your clinicians can act on. Patient, carrier or unaffected.

What your laboratory needs

A thermal cycler, a gel tank and a punch, and staff who have run PCR or have been trained. We help set the method up.

For public health programmes. Screen and confirm in one visit.

For district, state and national programmes: one finger-prick sample, room-temperature transport, a confirmed result, and newborns tested at birth.

  • People lost between screening and confirmation. One test screens and confirms, so no one waits for a second visit.

How a programme runs

Testing

Care

  1. 1 Collect

    A finger-prick sample on a card, in the community or at birth.

  2. 2 Transport

    To the nearest testing centre, at room temperature.

  3. 3 Test

    Extraction-free molecular testing with LitLife™.

  4. 4 Report

    Results recorded against each sample in the app.

  5. 5 Treat and counsel

    Treatment, with genetic and social counselling for patients and carriers.

  6. 6 Follow up

    Awareness programmes and patient support groups.

For CSR and NGOs. Fund screening that ends in a diagnosis, not a referral.

Your support reaches a confirmed result, counselling and follow-up for every family.

Why it matters

  • Each rupee reaches an answer. Screening that stops at a possible case helps no one; one test that confirms does.

  • A camp both explains and tests. Community screening and awareness camps are run together.

  • Families are not left alone with a result. Every family a programme finds is counselled and supported.

Questions funders ask

We are an NGO with no laboratory. Can we still run screening?

Yes. Cards travel at room temperature to a nearby testing centre, so the laboratory need not be yours. Tell us where you plan to screen and we will say how the samples would be tested.

Do you work with CSR funders?

Yes. Most population-level screening is funded that way or by government. Tell us the community and the condition, and we will propose a programme.

What happens to the families a programme identifies?

Counselling for patients and carriers, a path to treatment where it exists, and support groups. That is part of the programme, not an extra.

How a partnership starts. Four steps, from the first conversation.

You bring the condition you need to screen for or diagnose. We bring the test, the method, the training and the counselling that follows a result.

  1. 1 You tell us the need

    The condition and the setting: who will be tested, roughly how many, where samples will be collected and which laboratory will run them.

  2. 2 We agree the scope

    What the test must detect, how results will be reported, and what training and counselling the programme needs.

  3. 3 We develop or adapt the test

    A test we have, or a new one for the mutations your population carries.

  4. 4 We train and stay with you

    Through reporting, counselling and follow-up.
