Spinal muscular atrophy, diagnosed while treatment can still help most.

LitLife™-SMA detects the SMN1 deletion behind most cases of SMA, from a dried blood spot. It is built for newborn screening, before symptoms, when treatment can do most.

  • Dried blood spot
  • No DNA extraction
  • Built for newborn screening
About the condition

SMA destroys the nerve cells that control muscles. Most cases come from losing both copies of the SMN1 gene. Treatments now exist, and they work best when given early, often before symptoms, which makes an early answer more valuable than ever.

About 1 in 40 adults carries one faulty copy without knowing, and a study in north India found closer to 1 in 30.

LitLife™-SMA. What it detects.

A screening and confirmatory test for spinal muscular atrophy, designed for newborn screening.

  • The SMN1 deletion

    Detects the homozygous SMN1 exon 7 deletion behind most cases of SMA.

  • Built for newborns

    Designed for newborn screening, where finding SMA before symptoms changes what treatment can do.

Who it is for

  • Newborn screening programmes

  • Maternity hospitals and paediatric units

  • Paediatric neurologists and the laboratories that serve them

Why a DNA test here. Where today's testing stops.

  1. 1

    Babies with weakness are often thought to be undernourished.

  2. 2

    Families can wait months or years for a diagnosis, and testing is mostly in a few city laboratories.

  3. 3

    By the time weakness is noticed, nerve cells have already been lost; treatment works best before symptoms.

In the laboratory, with the kit.

  • The sample

    A spot of blood, dried on a card. Minimally invasive.

  • The chemistry

    Direct PCR, optimised for dried blood spots. No DNA extraction is required.

    Rapid turnaround, high-throughput performance and an easy workflow.
