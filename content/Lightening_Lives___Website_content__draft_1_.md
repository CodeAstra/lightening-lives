## What Lightening Lives does

Lightening Lives develops genetic tests that are simple, affordable and usable outside specialised laboratories, so molecular diagnosis can reach everyone, including underserved communities.

Genetic tests have traditionally needed large equipment, specialised laboratories and trained staff, so they rarely reach the people who need them most. Lightening Lives designs its tests the other way round. Sample collection is simple and minimally invasive, results come back quickly, and the tests are designed so that collecting, processing and reading them doesn't call for specialised training, large equipment or a specialised laboratory.

The company's distinguishing strength is developing genetic tests that meet the REASSURED criteria. It has developed tests for national health programmes of the Government of India, including the National Sickle Cell Anaemia Elimination Mission.

**Mission (client's words, lightly edited):** To make molecular diagnosis accessible to all by developing innovative REASSURED genetic tests, with simple, affordable, minimally invasive sample collection and a quick turnaround for results.

**How the company describes itself:** Lightening Lives is an innovation-driven company developing REASSURED genetic tests, making molecular diagnosis accessible to all.

### The REASSURED approach

REASSURED is a published set of criteria for diagnostic tests that work in low-resource settings. It was introduced in 2019 as an update to the earlier ASSURED criteria from the World Health Organization's tropical disease research programme.

| Letter | Criterion | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R | Real-time connectivity | Results can be read or shared digitally with decision-makers |
| E | Ease of specimen collection | Samples are simple and minimally invasive to collect |
| A | Affordable | Affordable for the people tested and for the health system |
| S | Sensitive | Few false negatives |
| S | Specific | Few false positives |
| U | User-friendly | Simple to perform, with minimal training |
| R | Rapid and robust | Quick results; tests survive transport and storage without special conditions |
| E | Equipment-free or simple | No special equipment, or only simple devices |
| D | Deliverable to end-users | Reaches the people who need it most |

The company confirms it uses REASSURED. Wherever the acronym is spelled out, use all nine criteria above; older materials such as the SMA poster say "ASSURED" and list only seven.

## The tests

So far, Lightening Lives has developed REASSURED genetic tests for five groups of inherited conditions, for use in national health programmes. These are its current tests, not the limit of what it does: the company can develop tests for other genetic conditions too, so the website should present these five as the current range, with room for more. The condition descriptions below are plain-language background the website can draw on.

### Sickle cell anaemia: LitLife™-SCA (flagship product)

**The test:** LitLife™-SCA is a trademarked, simple dried blood spot test for detecting sickle cell anaemia. It was developed for the National Sickle Cell Anaemia Elimination Mission and is validated by ICMR. Lightening Lives has an MOU with CSIR-CCMB for the development and commercialisation of this assay.

**The condition:** Sickle cell disease is an inherited blood disorder in which red blood cells become rigid and sickle-shaped, slowing or blocking blood flow and causing pain, anaemia, infections and organ damage. In India it is most common among tribal communities. The Government of India launched the Elimination Mission in July 2023, aiming to eliminate sickle cell disease as a public health problem by 2047.

### Thalassaemia

**The condition:** Thalassaemia is an inherited blood disorder in which the body makes too little haemoglobin. People with the severe form, thalassaemia major, need regular blood transfusions throughout life. Carriers are usually healthy and often unaware of their status; when both parents are carriers, each pregnancy has a 1 in 4 chance of thalassaemia major. Genetic testing supports carrier screening, diagnosis and family counselling.

### Spinal muscular atrophy (SMA)

**The test (from the SMA campaign):** a dried blood spot sample, high sensitivity and specificity, rapid turnaround, and adaptable and affordable for large-scale newborn screening. Campaign line: Early detection. Timely care. Better outcomes.

**The condition:** SMA is a rare inherited neuromuscular disorder that damages the nerve cells controlling muscles, causing progressive weakness that affects movement and, in severe forms, breathing and swallowing. Treatments now exist and work best when started early, ideally before symptoms appear, which is why newborn screening matters.

### Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD)

**The condition:** DMD is an inherited muscle disorder that mainly affects boys. The body cannot make dystrophin, a protein muscles need, so muscles weaken progressively from early childhood. Diagnosis often comes late; an earlier genetic diagnosis allows timely care and lets families find out whether relatives such as mothers and sisters are carriers.

### Blood coagulation disorders

**The condition:** In inherited bleeding disorders such as haemophilia, blood does not clot properly because a clotting factor is missing or low. Haemophilia A and B mainly affect males; women can be carriers. Genetic testing confirms the diagnosis, identifies carriers and supports family counselling and care planning.

What each test detects (and whether every test uses a dried blood spot) is still to be confirmed; see Content guardrails.

## Who they serve and how visitors engage

Lightening Lives works only with institutions, and expects collaborations and partnerships to be the main way website visitors engage.

### Who can work with them

- **Hospitals, laboratories and clinicians** (the client's most important audience)
- **District health officers** and government health programmes
- **NGOs and CSR funders**, typically for large, population-level requirements
- Regulatory and statutory bodies such as ICMR and CDSCO may also visit the site to check the company's credentials.

**Not served:** families or individuals looking for a test. All orders are institutional or regulatory, in bulk. The website should make this clear and politely point individuals to their doctor or nearest government health facility.

### What visitors should be able to do (client's order of priority)

1. Explore a collaboration or partnership (primary)
2. Request a test (the client also says "request a kit"; see Content guardrails)
3. Ask about awareness and counselling programmes
4. Reach the sales team

**Kept off the website:** pricing, minimum order quantities and procurement details. These are discussed per request, based on institutional or regulatory requirements.

### Awareness and counselling programmes

Lightening Lives runs awareness and counselling programmes, mostly on behalf of the government. Awareness programmes explain inherited conditions and screening to communities; counselling helps people and families understand results and carrier status. Full details will follow from the client.

## Content guardrails

These are rules about what the website says, not how it looks; they protect the company's credibility with clinicians, programme officers and regulators.

### Do

- Describe Lightening Lives as a healthcare technology company that develops tests, not as a charity, NGO, diagnostics lab or "diagnostic tool".
- Name the sample precisely: "dried blood spot" or "a few drops of blood on a card".
- Put proof beside claims: ICMR validation, the CSIR-CCMB collaboration, national programmes.
- Make clear that tests are for institutions only.
- Use Indian English spelling: anaemia, thalassaemia, haemophilia, programme, organisation.

### Don't

- Show prices, procurement figures or clinical performance figures.
- Show identifiable people in photos.
- Use "single drop" or "one drop of blood", or hype such as "endless possibilities" or "revolutionary". This framing echoes Theranos, which claimed hundreds of tests from a few drops of blood on an unproven device; precise, proven language keeps clear of that association.
- Call sample collection "painless"; a finger prick isn't. Use "minimally invasive".
- Use the ICMR logo or partner logos without permission.

### Claims to confirm before publishing

- **Tests or kits:** the client says it sells tests, not kits, but also mentions "request a kit" and calls LitLife™-SCA a kit.
- **LitLife™-SCA:** ™ or ®; whether it detects disease, carrier status or both; exact wording of the ICMR validation.
- **Test scope:** what the thalassaemia, DMD and coagulation tests detect, and whether all use dried blood spots.
- **Usability claims:** "no specialised training, equipment or laboratory" must match each test's instructions for use and regulatory status.
