# Lightening Lives: landing page

Eleven design options for the Lightening Lives homepage, each built as a production page, with a comparison toolbar so the client can switch between them and try variations before choosing. **Design 11 · Lumen (`lumen.html`) was chosen for production on 13 October 2026**; `DESIGN.md` describes it, and "Going live" below says what to do with the file.

## View it

Open `index.html` in a browser; no server is needed. For live rebuilds while editing:

```sh
npm install
npm run dev      # http://localhost:4173
```

## The comparison toolbar

The dark bar above each page comes from `assets/compare/compare.js`. It offers:

- **Design**: switch between the ten pages. A design chosen here always opens as designed, with its own typography, background and ink selected in the controls; variations tried on one design are not carried to another.
- **Typography, Background, Accent**: the first option is always the design as drawn. For designs 01 and 02 the others are the other direction's typefaces and colours, plus two tones taken from the logo and the Daylight palette. Design 03 offers a regular-width cut of its typeface, a pale yellow or green-white background, and a lighter green ink. Design 04 offers sans-serif headings, a pale sun or pale leaf background, and maroon ink; the colours of the four DNA letters stay as they are, because they carry meaning. Design 05 offers Mukta or Familjen Grotesk headings, a pale sun or pale leaf background, and deep green ink; the emblem keeps the logo's own colours. Design 06 offers IBM Plex Sans or Familjen Grotesk headings, a white or pale brass background, and deep green ink; the model's metal, glass and sun colours are not varied. Design 07 offers Design 01's or Design 02's typefaces, a white or butter background, and maroon or rust ink; its suns keep their own shades. Design 08 offers Design 01's or Design 02's typefaces, a pale sun or pale leaf background, and maroon or deep green ink; its yellow stays, because it is what shows which part of the film is playing. Design 09 offers Design 03's Anek Latin or Familjen Grotesk in place of Quicksand and Nunito, Design 03's backgrounds, and Design 03's deep green in place of leaf green. Design 10 offers Design 09's variations and one more, **Film**: the opening film at its designed pace (6.5 seconds), quicker (5.5) or unhurried (8, which is what the design was before the user chose the quicker pace on 12 October 2026). Choosing one replays the film, so the three can be compared. **Drop** does the same for the drop of blood: small, as designed (its radius 0.036 of the card's length, since the user asked for it smaller on 13 October 2026), larger (1.4 times, the size before that) or smaller still (0.75). Design 11 offers Design 10's Quicksand and Nunito, or Familjen Grotesk, in place of Exo and Roboto Mono; white or Design 10's pale green in place of ice white; leaf green or slate in place of DNA blue; **Highlight**, the colour of what is being pointed out (sun amber as designed, a softer marigold, coral, or the life green the theme had until 13 October 2026; the pool of light, the map, its key and the film follow it); **Mark**, how a name is marked (the bar under it as designed, Design 10's box round it, a stroke under the lower half of the words, or the name in DNA blue with no amber); and the same choices of film pace and drop size. A design with six or more controls folds the bar into its "Customise" panel below 1640px, where they no longer fit in one line.
- **Copy link**: every choice is mirrored in the URL, so a link reproduces exactly what is on screen and a reload keeps it. Variations are not remembered between visits: opening a design without them in the link shows it as designed.
- **Reset** and **Hide**.

Variations only override the page's CSS custom properties; "as designed" sets nothing.

## Edit it

Sources live in `src/`; the pages load compiled files from `assets/css` and `assets/js`, which are committed so the site runs without a build.

```sh
npm run build    # Tailwind CSS v4 + esbuild
```

- `src/css/<design>.css`: design tokens (`:root`) and component styles for that design.
- `src/js/sample-card/india-map.js`: the hero map (three.js). It is bundled separately and loaded after first paint; without WebGL the flat map in `assets/img/india-map-*.svg` stays in place.
- `src/js/sample-card/india-data.js`: state outlines and the map's three layers. Edit the `SICKLE_BELT`, `SICKLE`, `THAL` and `PLACES` lists there if the programme data changes. On the sickle cell layer the five states the central belt runs through stand tall and the other mission focus states only just lift; `assets/img/india-map-sickle.svg`, the flat fallback, is coloured to match by hand. Designs 01 and 07 share this map.
- `src/js/scale/scene.js`: design 03's 3D scene (three.js, bundled separately as `scale-scene.js`). Camera stops, card proportions, timings and where the card sits across the page's column are constants at the top of the file; every colour is read from the page's CSS custom properties. The card is modelled on the company's own (cover flap with the logo, one collection circle, barcoded pocket) and drawn in code; the logo is inlined into the bundle so the scene also works from `file://`. Every card prints its own barcode and six-digit code (`SCM / 100001` on the card the camera follows, a generated one on each of the others); they are illustrative, not real sample codes.
- `src/js/scale/india-cells.js`: India as a grid of cells, one card per cell, plus the four highlighted places.
- `assets/img/scale-*.webp`: still images of design 03's camera stops. They replace the live scene for visitors without WebGL or who have asked for reduced motion. If the scene's look changes, capture them again.
- `src/js/strand/scene.js`: design 04's 3D scene (three.js, bundled separately as `strand-scene.js`). The strand's proportions, which tiles are marked (`MARKS` for the tests so far, `OPEN` for the open place), the camera stops for wide and narrow screens, and the load animation are constants at the top of the file; colours and the typeface on the tiles are read from the page's CSS custom properties. The tiles are drawn as textured card with pressed letters, lit as a small model in a studio would be, and seen through a shallow depth of field; on a machine that cannot keep up, the depth-of-field pass switches itself off before anything else is reduced. The letters on the strand are illustrative, generated from a fixed seed; they are not a real sequence.
- `src/js/strand/main.js`: the page's own behaviour (the mobile menu, and loading the scene). The names on the five marked tiles are the `[data-pin]` items in `strand.html`; change them there if the range changes.
- `assets/img/strand-*.webp`: still images of design 04's three camera stops, used the same way as design 03's.
- `src/js/tree/scene.js`: design 05's 3D scene (three.js, bundled separately as `tree-scene.js`). The emblem's proportions (`SUN`, `LEAVES`, the figure's outline), the new leaf that opens at the second stop (`BUD`), the camera stops for wide and narrow screens, and the arrival sequence are constants at the top of the file; every colour is read from the page's CSS custom properties. The parts are drawn as textured card at different depths in front of the page, and the key light follows the pointer, so the shadows move with it. The names on the five leaves are the `[data-pin]` items in `tree.html`.
- `assets/img/tree-*.webp`: still images of design 05's three camera stops, used the same way as design 03's.
- `src/js/helix/scene.js`: design 06's 3D scene (three.js, bundled separately as `helix-scene.js`). The model (`RUNGS`, `RISE`, `TWIST`, `BASE`), the five lit rungs (`MARKS`) and the open one above them (`OPEN`), the lights, the lens and the four camera stops for wide and narrow screens are constants at the top of the file; every colour is read from the page's CSS custom properties. On a machine that cannot keep up it gives up depth of field first, then the glass's refraction, then pixels. The names on the five lit rungs are the `[data-pin]` items and the `.five` list in `helix.html`.
- `assets/img/helix-*.webp`: still images of design 06's four camera stops, used the same way as design 03's. They are baked on the design's pearl grey, so with another background chosen from the toolbar the fallback shows a faint grey edge round them.
- `sun-card.html` is generated from `sample-card.html`, so the two stay the same in structure and copy. The generator is not in the repo; edit `sun-card.html` directly, and carry over by hand any copy change made to Design 01. Its suns are four SVG symbols at the top of the page (`#sun-solid`, `#sun-lines`, `#sun-dots`, `#sun-open`), coloured where they are used through `data-tone`; the shades are the `--butter`, `--apricot`, `--blush`, `--sage` and `--noon` tokens in `src/css/sun-card.css`.
- `src/js/sun-card/main.js`: design 07's behaviour. It is Design 01's, plus the suns: they turn a little as the page scrolls, the hero's leans toward the pointer and changes shade with the map's layer. The hero map itself is Design 01's (`assets/js/india-map.js`), unchanged.
- `src/js/drop/scene.js`: design 09's 3D scene (three.js, bundled separately as `drop-scene.js`). It began as a copy of design 03's and is kept separately so that either can change without the other. What is its own: the falling drop (`DROPS`: when it is let go, where it lands and its share of the spot, as a list so more can be added; `DROP`: size, height, and how long it takes to fall and soak in), the two map stops seen from straight above (`STOPS`), the map's colouring, the pins on the places the company works, the ripple when the layer changes (`RIPPLE`), and the borders and names, which come up only on the step where the reader chooses a layer (`LINES`). On a narrow screen the text lies under the map, so `fitMap` measures the room each map step's text leaves and fits the map into it; below `NAMES_FROM` pixels to a map unit the map is too small to carry names, and only a picked state is named. The borders are an SVG laid over the canvas, moved and scaled each frame to stay on the map; they take the pointer, so a state is picked out by its true outline. `NAME_NUDGE` (and `NAME_NUDGE_NARROW`, for small screens) moves a name where neighbours' names would collide. The code printed on the cards is set in the page's text typeface, whatever the toolbar has chosen.
- `src/js/drop/map-layers.js`: for each layer of design 09's map, its name, how strongly it marks each state, the key, and what it says about a state that is picked out. The sentence shown for each layer is the `data-readout` on its button in `drop.html`. Written by hand: edit the lists here if the programme data changes. They are the same lists as design 01's map uses (`src/js/sample-card/india-data.js`); change both together.
- `src/js/drop/india-cells.js`, `india-states.js`, `india-borders.js`: design 03's grid of card cells with the state each cell lies in; the states' codes, names and label points; and each state's outline as an SVG path. All three are generated from the state outlines in design 01's map data; the generator is not in the repo.
- `src/js/drop/main.js`: design 09's page behaviour, including the map's sentence, key and state panel. The sentence and key work without the 3D scene, so they still answer where the page falls back to stills. The scene tells the page which state is picked out on the map with an `ll:map-state` event.
- `assets/img/drop-*.webp`: still images of design 09's camera stops (the last, `drop-programme.webp`, with the company's places pinned), of its map (with borders and names) for the sickle cell and thalassaemia layers, and of the unused card. They replace the live scene for visitors without WebGL or who have asked for reduced motion, and there the map's three choices swap between stills. If the scene's look changes, capture them again.
- `src/js/reel/scene.js`: design 10's film (three.js, bundled separately as `reel-scene.js`). The card, its print and the field of cards are design 09's; the rest is its own. Everything on screen is worked out from the film's time alone, which is what makes pausing, replaying and jumping simple. The film's timing is two tables. `TAKE` is the camera's one take: each row is where the camera is at a given second (how much it shows, how high it looks from, which way it faces), and between rows it follows a smooth curve through them (`curve`, a monotone cubic), so it has no stops: it is already moving at the first row and comes to rest only on the last. Do not put two rows with the same framing next to each other unless a rest is wanted there. `TIMING` is everything else: when the cover opens and shuts, how long the drop swells, when it lets go, how long it takes to fall (`release` + `fall` is the second it lands, which is where the camera's second row is: move them together), be drawn into the paper and soak, when the places come up on the map and are named, when each caption comes up, and when the film is over. To make the film quicker or slower, change the seconds in both. `DROP` sets the drop's size and the height it hangs from. Like a video, the film keeps its own time: it keeps to real time on slow machines (down to eight frames a second), and scrolling it off screen does not stop it (it is only not drawn until it is back); the reader's pause button and a hidden tab are the only things that hold it. Whether the film uses its wide or its small-screen framing is the stylesheet's decision (the wide one is the one in which `.hero-frame` is shown), so the two cannot disagree. For checking it and capturing stills, `window.llFilm.show(seconds)` holds the film at a moment, and `window.llFilm.timing` and `window.llFilm.take` are the tables themselves.
- `src/js/reel/main.js`: design 10's page behaviour: the menu, the REASSURED word, loading the film, and the map. The map is drawn in SVG from design 09's data (`src/js/drop/india-borders.js`, `india-states.js`, `india-cells.js` and `map-layers.js`, imported, not copied, so the facts are kept in one place). Each state is filled by how strongly the chosen layer marks it; pointing at a state, tapping it, or stepping to it with the arrow keys shows what the map knows about it.
- `reel.html`'s head script decides, before first paint, whether the film can play (WebGL) and whether it should start by itself (no request for reduced motion). A reader who has asked for reduced motion gets a still and a "Play the film" button; without WebGL the page shows the still alone. The same text as the film's captions is set out, with more to say, under "How it works".
- `assets/img/reel-*.webp`: four stills from design 10's film: the drop above the card (also the film's stand-in), the soaked spot, the batch, and the map with its pins. The soaked spot and the batch were taken from an earlier cut, in which the camera rested over each; the film now passes both without stopping, a little higher over the batch. To capture new ones: hide the toolbar and the hero's text, hold the film with `window.llFilm.show()` at about 1.5, 2.65 and 3.9 seconds and at its end, in an 1800 by 1000 window, and crop a 1000-pixel square from x = 712.
- `src/css/lumen.css`: design 11's stylesheet, generated from `reel.css`: that file with Lumen's token values, and one block headed "Lumen's material" at the end of the components layer that holds the whole of the difference. The colour tokens keep design 10's names (`--mist`, `--meadow`, `--sun` and so on), because the film and the toolbar read them. The material block adds the pools of light (a radial gradient behind each `bg-paper` band, DNA blue from the left or sunlight from the right in turn, clipped to the band), the glass (one rule listing every surface made of it), the label voice in Roboto Mono (its weight and tracking are tokens, `--label-weight` and `--label-tracking`, so the toolbar's Quicksand and Nunito choice can set design 10's), DNA blue for the solid button and the chosen map layer, the bar of `--sun` under named proof (an underline 0.14em thick that does not skip descenders, in place of design 10's filled box, so that a name that wraps does not stack into a slab; `--mark-*` properties let the toolbar show the other ways tried), and the map key's swatches mixed through paper. It changes one breakpoint (the emblem stands alone in the header up to 78rem, not 73rem, because the links wrapped beside the name in between) and no size, position or border; a diff against `reel.css` shows only those additions and the token values.
- `src/js/lumen/`: copies of design 10's `main.js` and `scene.js`, kept separately so that either design can change without the other, as design 09's scene is kept apart from design 03's. Two differences in the film: the code printed on the cards is set in `--ff-mono` where the page defines it, so on design 11 it is Roboto Mono; and a marked card is lightened towards white before it is warmed to `--sun`, because amber mixed straight into the blue field goes through khaki. The map on the page (`main.js`) mixes its states and paints its key's swatches by the same rule.
- `assets/img/lumen-*.webp`: design 11's stills, captured the same way as design 10's: the drop above the card (the film's stand-in), the soaked spot, the batch and the map with its pins; `lumen-blank.webp`, the open card before the drop (the film at 0.3 s), for the contact section; and `lumen-map-sickle.webp` and `lumen-map-thal.webp`, the drawn map under each layer, shown until the map is live. `lumen-social.png` (1200 by 630) is the preview card for shared links: the opening at the film's end, captured at 1440 wide. All were captured again on 13 October 2026 in sun amber.
- `src/js/film/main.js`: design 08's behaviour. It keeps the headline in step with the film, runs the sun-shaped play and pause button, pauses the film while it is off screen, and marks the section being read in the header. The film's three parts are the three `[data-mark]` buttons in `film.html`: `data-at` is the second each part begins (half-way through the cross-fade into it) and `data-still` is its still image. `data-wrap` on the video is the second from which the end of the loop is already fading back into the first part. Recut the film and these four numbers are all that change.
- `assets/video/`: design 08's film at two sizes, and a still from each of its three parts. Sources, licences and what was changed are in `assets/video/CREDITS.md`. The film plays by itself, muted, only for visitors who have not asked for reduced motion or data saving; they get the still and a play button, and nothing is downloaded until they press it. The page works without its script: the film then plays with the browser's own controls.
- In designs 03 to 06 and 09 the story's text and its 3D subject keep to the same centred column as the rest of the page (`--frame` and `--gutter` in each stylesheet), and on monitors wider than a laptop's the whole page scales up with the window (`html { font-size }`).

## Publish to GitHub Pages

```sh
npm run pages                      # builds, then copies only the site into dist/
npx gh-pages --dist dist --dotfiles   # pushes dist/ to the gh-pages branch
```

All paths are relative, so the site works from a `/<repo>/` subpath. Publishing `dist/` rather than the whole branch matters: Pages serves every file it is given, and this repo also holds the internal brief in `content/`.

## Going live with the chosen design

The chosen design is `lumen.html`. Its page is complete; these are the steps that turn the comparison build into the site.

1. Rename `lumen.html` to `index.html` (replacing the redirect page) and delete the other designs, their stylesheets and scripts, and the stills they alone use.
2. Delete the line marked `Comparison toolbar` in its `<head>`, and the `assets/compare` folder.
3. Delete the `noindex` robots line marked `Comparison build`, and add `<link rel="canonical" href="https://lighteninglives.in/">` once the site is at that address. The preview card (`og:image`) already points at `https://lighteninglives.in/assets/img/lumen-social.png`; keep that file name, or change both.
4. Point the nav, "Privacy policy" and "Terms" links at the real pages as they are built. For now the nav scrolls to sections of the landing page and enquiry buttons open an email to `admin@lighteninglives.in`.
5. Add a `404.html` for GitHub Pages (built from the same header, column and footer, as `DESIGN.md` says under "Other pages"), and swap the three representative field photos for the client's own (see below).

## Content still to settle

- **Thalassaemia on the maps (designs 01, 07 and 09) was checked against sources on 11 October 2026**, and Delhi was added. The maps now mark Punjab, Delhi, Gujarat, Maharashtra and West Bengal as places where higher carrier frequencies are reported, and say that thalassaemia is found across India. What that rests on: the National Health Mission's *Guidelines for Prevention and Control of Hemoglobinopathies in India* (2016) give an average carrier frequency of 3 to 4% across the country, higher in "certain communities, such as Sindhis, Punjabis, Gujaratis, Bengalis, Mahars, Kolis, Saraswats, Lohanas and Gaurs"; a six-city study (Mohanty et al., *J Community Genet*, 2013) found the highest frequencies in Ludhiana (3.96%) and Kolkata (3.64%), with Vadodara 2.68%, Mumbai 2.55%, Bengaluru 2.16% and Dibrugarh 1.48%; and a schools study (Madan et al., *Indian J Hum Genet*, 2010) found 5.47% in Delhi against 2.68% in Mumbai. Two cautions for the client. Frequencies differ more between communities than between states, so the maps say "reported in", never "highest in". And Gujarat and Maharashtra are marked for their communities and for particular districts: their city-wide figures in the six-city study are close to the national average.
